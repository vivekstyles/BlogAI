A good side hustle for someone with a full-time job is not the one with the highest possible income. It is one that fits into **evenings and weekends**, does not need you at 2 p.m. on a Tuesday, does not conflict with your employer, and still pays after costs and taxes. This guide covers **15 side hustles with a track record** (each one has established platforms, clear demand and many people doing it today) and rates them on exactly those constraints.

**The short answer.** If you have a professional skill, freelancing it in the evenings usually pays best per hour. If you want to earn without selling yourself, flexible gigs such as tutoring, pet care and delivery are the easiest to start. Whatever you choose, check your employment contract first and set aside money for taxes from the first payment.

**How we wrote this.** We use the word "proven" to mean an established type of work with real platforms and customers, **not** proof that you will earn a particular amount. Platform fees and IRS rules were checked on official pages on 25 October 2026. The delivery-driver example is our own calculation with the assumptions shown. Tax rules are U.S.-specific; this is general education, not financial, tax or legal advice.

## Why people are taking on side work

In the Federal Reserve's latest survey of U.S. households, **17%** of adults said they had worked more or taken another job in the prior 12 months in response to higher prices ([Federal Reserve, May 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2026-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2025-income-and-expenses.htm)). The same survey found that **58%** of self-employed adults said their income varied from month to month. That is the trade-off with side work: flexibility in exchange for uneven pay. Keep your day job's paycheck for fixed bills, and treat side income as money for goals, debt payoff or savings.

## Check these before you start

**Your employment contract.** Many contracts have clauses on outside work, conflicts of interest, confidentiality, non-solicitation of clients and ownership of anything you create. Do not use your employer's equipment, time or confidential information for side work, and do not compete with your employer or take its clients. If in doubt, ask HR or a lawyer before you start, not after.

**Taxes.** Net self-employment earnings of **\$400 or more** in a year trigger self-employment tax of **15.3%** ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/self-employment-tax-social-security-and-medicare-taxes)). You generally need to pay estimated tax if you expect to owe **\$1,000 or more** when you file. If you have a salaried job, there is an easier route: file a new **Form W-4** with your employer and have extra tax withheld from your paycheck to cover the side income. You avoid an underpayment penalty if you have paid at least **90%** of this year's tax or **100%** of last year's, whichever is smaller (higher-income taxpayers face a higher prior-year percentage; see IRS Publication 505) ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/estimated-taxes)).

**Insurance.** Driving for pay, caring for children or pets, and working in clients' homes all carry liability risks that a personal policy may exclude. Ask your insurer before you start and check what cover the platform provides.

## The 15 side hustles

### 1. Freelance your day-job skill

Writing, design, development, data analysis, marketing, HR, accounting and project management all sell as freelance services. Because clients pay for professional experience, this usually earns the most per hour of anything on this list. Find clients outside your employer's market, keep work strictly on your own devices and time, and agree deadlines that allow for your day job's busy weeks.

### 2. Tutoring

Parents look for tutors in the evenings and on weekends, which matches your free time. School subjects, exam preparation, languages and music are common; online tutoring removes travel time. Local parent groups and school noticeboards often bring better-paying private students than large platforms.

### 3. Food and grocery delivery

Delivery apps let you log in whenever you want, which makes them the most schedule-friendly option here. The catch is costs: fuel, wear and tear, insurance and depreciation come out of your pay, and the platform's per-order pay changes with demand. See the worked example below before deciding.

### 4. Rideshare driving

Rideshare driving has similar flexibility, with stricter vehicle and background requirements. Evenings and weekends are often busier. The same cost logic applies, and personal auto policies may not cover you while you are working, so check your insurance.

### 5. Pet sitting and dog walking

Owners need dog walks during the workday, which may not fit, but **overnight sitting, weekend boarding and evening visits** do. Platforms take a service fee from sitters; Rover's UK help page, for example, says sitters keep 85% of each booking and notes that rates vary by country and by when your profile was approved ([Rover](https://support-uk.rover.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360000909143)). Check your own account's fee and your lease or local rules before boarding pets at home.

### 6. Babysitting

Parents need sitters for evenings out and weekends, exactly when you are free. A pediatric first-aid and CPR course makes you more trustworthy and is worth the cost. Regular families give steady weekly income without a platform cut.

### 7. Handyman and odd jobs

Furniture assembly, mounting TVs, moving help, yard work and small repairs are in steady demand, and task apps or neighborhood groups can fill your Saturdays. Electrical, plumbing and gas work often require a license, and larger jobs may require a contractor license in your state; stick to what you are allowed to do.

### 8. Flipping secondhand items

Buy underpriced furniture, electronics, tools or clothing at garage sales and thrift stores, clean or fix them, and resell online. You set your own hours and can batch photography and listing into one evening a week. Start small, track every purchase, and learn sold prices in one category before expanding.

### 9. Bookkeeping for small businesses

Monthly bookkeeping can be done any evening before the client's deadline, which makes it a near-ideal fit for a full-time job. It needs accuracy and some training; bookkeeping software certifications are a common credential. A few steady clients can provide predictable monthly income.

### 10. Fitness instruction

Gyms and studios need instructors for early-morning and evening classes, outside typical office hours. You usually need a recognized certification and liability insurance. Teaching two or three classes a week is a common side arrangement.

### 11. Event work

Catering, bartending, ushering, stage crew, sports venues and festivals hire for evening and weekend shifts through staffing agencies. Shifts are irregular and seasonal, and some roles (such as serving alcohol) require a local permit or training. Agencies often pay as employees, so tax is withheld.

### 12. Event and portrait photography

Weddings, parties, graduations and family portraits happen on weekends. You need a capable camera, a portfolio and contracts that set out deliverables and payment terms. Editing takes as long as shooting, so price for both.

### 13. Virtual assistant across time zones

If you work days in one time zone, your evenings may be business hours elsewhere. Inbox management, scheduling, research and customer support for overseas small businesses is a practical fit. Agree response-time expectations in writing.

### 14. A newsletter, blog or channel

Content businesses fit any schedule but pay slowest, typically months before the first income. Choose a topic you know well enough to write about weekly for a year. See ways 19–23 in our guide to [making money online](/articles/make-money-online-for-beginners) for how each format earns.

### 15. Paid studies and usability tests

Research studies and usability tests take minutes, not hours, and can be done any evening. Pay is modest (Prolific sets a minimum of £6 or \$8 per hour for researchers to pay participants, and recommends £9 or \$12 ([Prolific](https://researcher-help.prolific.com/en/articles/445266-how-much-should-i-pay-participants))), so treat it as a top-up, not a plan.

## What a "\$22 an hour" gig really pays

Delivery and rideshare pay is often quoted gross. Here is one hour of delivery work under assumptions we chose for illustration: **\$22 gross pay** and **15 business miles** driven.

- **Car costs.** The IRS standard mileage rate, which is meant to cover fuel, maintenance, insurance and depreciation, is **76 cents per mile** for business driving from 1 July 2026 (72.5 cents before that) ([IRS Announcement 2026-11](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2026-29_irb)). 15 miles × \$0.76 = **\$11.40**.
- **Self-employment tax.** Applied to 92.35% of the \$10.60 left: about **\$1.50**.
- **Left before income tax:** about **\$9.10** per hour.

The IRS rate overstates the cash cost of a paid-off, fuel-efficient car and understates it for an expensive one, but it is a reasonable estimate of what driving really costs. Two lessons: track your miles (they are deductible for tax), and compare gigs on what you keep per hour, not what the app shows.

## How to make a side hustle sustainable

- **Set a weekly hour cap.** Five to ten hours is sustainable alongside full-time work for most people; more than that tends to hurt sleep, health and your main job.
- **Give the money a destination.** A named goal (an emergency fund, a debt, a down payment) keeps you motivated. Our guide to [25 money management tips](/articles/money-management-tips) suggests an order of priorities.
- **Keep records from day one.** A separate bank account, receipts and a mileage log make tax time painless.
- **Review after three months.** Compare hours, true hourly pay and how you feel. Drop or change what is not working.

## FAQ

### What is the best side hustle for someone with a full-time job?

The one that uses a skill you already have and runs on your own schedule. For professionals that is usually freelancing; for others, tutoring, babysitting, pet sitting or flexible delivery work are easy to start.

### How many hours should a side hustle take?

There is no rule, but five to ten hours a week is a common, sustainable range. Track your energy and your day-job performance as well as your earnings.

### Do I need to tell my employer about my side hustle?

It depends on your contract and local law. Read your employment agreement and handbook for outside-work, conflict-of-interest and intellectual-property clauses. When a clause applies or you are unsure, ask before starting.

### How do I pay taxes on side hustle income?

In the U.S., report it on your return (usually Schedule C), and either make quarterly estimated payments or increase withholding at your day job with a new Form W-4 ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/estimated-taxes)). Keep records of income and deductible expenses such as business mileage.
